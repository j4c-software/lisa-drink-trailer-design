## How every project chat must use this resource

1. Treat values marked **CONFIRMED** as usable design inputs.
2. Treat values marked **WORKING** as concept-only assumptions. Do not issue fabrication drawings, order fitted components, cut the body, or calculate final weights from them.
3. Treat values marked **VERIFY** as required measurements or decisions.
4. Record every new verified value in the Master Data Record below, including its source and date.
5. Never substitute dimensions from another Circle J trailer. Circle J two-horse trailers varied by year, roof, floor, axle, tack-room and warmblood configuration.
6. Obtain HCD and health-plan approval before construction or equipment installation. Structural openings must be reviewed by the responsible fabricator/engineer before cutting.
7. Use the locked concept-design envelope in Section 1A for all layouts, future authorized renderings, equipment studies and preliminary calculations. It may be changed only by an explicit revision to this specification and the Decisions Log.

## 1C. Locked exterior customer-experience requirements

**Decision status: APPROVED AND LOCKED FOR DESIGN.** Apply these requirements to the common exterior concept used with all three interior options.

### Service and customer flow

- Organize the single wide service opening with **order/POS at the customer’s right, bartender interaction/preparation at center and finished-drink pickup at the customer’s left**. This preserves the approved physical front/tongue-to-rear workflow and all approved elevations.
- Use small removable `ORDER` and `PICKUP` identifiers rather than additional openings or permanent projecting signs.
- Position a high-contrast removable menu so approaching guests can read it before reaching the transaction point and without blocking the active customer.
- Keep customer waste, cup return and water away from both the order and pickup ends; use a separate portable service point.

### Customer ledge and accessibility

- Provide a 14–16-inch-deep operating ledge with rounded exterior corners, an easy-clean matte or satin surface, controlled drip edge or groove, concealed folding supports and positive operating/travel latches.
- Bag hooks, if used, shall be recessed or folding and shall not create projections into customer knee space or the accessible route.
- Incorporate an accessible transaction section provisionally 36 inches long maximum 36 inches above the customer-side finished surface, with the same full ledge depth and clear adjacent floor space for a parallel approach. Final dimensions and applicability require accessibility review after the trailer and operating grade are known.
- Do not place fixed stools at the trailer ledge.

### Lighting

- **Lighting character reference:** the user-selected **“Sip the Style” precedent photograph** from the exterior precedent review.
- Match its soft, warm and layered hospitality atmosphere; do not imitate a brightly illuminated concession trailer.
- Use concealed, glare-controlled 2700–3000 K task light below the raised hatch, soft visible interior/back-bar illumination and portable low-level lighting at the seating area and accessible route.
- Aim fixtures away from customer faces; keep menus, drinks, hands and the POS clearly readable after dusk.

### Seating and setting

- Maintain a clear service apron directly in front of the window.
- Provide a short-wait zone with two or three portable standing cocktail tables.
- Place the main seating zone approximately 6–10 feet beyond and to one side of the service apron so seated guests do not become the queue.
- Include conventional dining-height seating, at least one wheelchair-usable table position and an optional small lounge cluster appropriate to weddings or private events.
- All tables, chairs, lounge furniture, rugs, planters, umbrellas and low-level lights are loose event equipment and shall not be shown attached to the trailer.

### Weather, branding and safety

- Use the raised top-hinged hatch as limited counter cover; provide a drip edge or gutter that directs water away from both counter ends.
- Use freestanding market umbrellas or a venue-supplied portable canopy for seating-area shade; no permanently attached fabric awning is included.
- Use restrained removable branding suitable for weddings, private events and temporary sponsor identities.
- Provide a level, slip-resistant customer service area with no exposed projecting brackets or utilities.
- Place shore-power inlet, water connections, tank servicing and mechanical vents on the non-customer side wherever engineering allows.
- Use discreet commercial stabilizers at the operating corners.

### Exterior exclusions

Do not include fixed trailer-mounted stools, permanent exterior bottle/glass shelving, customer trash directly below pickup, large roof signs, competing menu boards, decorative hardware that impedes cleaning, attached lounge furniture, attached umbrellas, or exposed equipment boxes on the customer-facing wall.

## 5. Weight, balance and towing design

### Required scale states

Obtain individual axle and tongue weights, with the trailer level, in these states:

1. As purchased
2. Fully stripped but structurally intact
3. Construction checkpoint before finishes
4. Final dry configuration
5. Maximum operating configuration: full potable water, maximum wastewater assumption, full ice/product, propane/fuel if any, batteries, supplies and loose equipment

### Controlled calculations

Maintain a component schedule with item, quantity, unit weight, total weight and longitudinal/lateral/vertical center of mass. Calculate:

- Final gross trailer weight
- Load on each axle and each tire
- Tongue weight and tongue-weight percentage
- Left/right imbalance
- Remaining payload reserve
- Tow vehicle payload consumed by tongue weight, hitch and occupants/cargo
- Braking and hitch compatibility

### Project targets

- Never exceed the lowest applicable GVWR, GAWR, tire, wheel, coupler, hitch, safety-chain or tow-vehicle rating.
- Use **10–15% tongue weight** only as an initial conventional bumper-pull target; confirm the final target through actual scale testing and the responsible engineer/hitch professional.
- Concept target: approximately 12–13% loaded tongue weight, with no configuration falling outside the approved stable range.
- Place tanks low and close to the axle group. Analyze both full and empty tank states.
- Do not use water, product or movable equipment as permanent ballast.
- Maintain design reserve rather than designing exactly to GVWR.
- Perform a documented loaded road test, brake-controller setup and sway assessment before service.

### San Francisco construction controls currently identified

- Plans must state the facility’s length, width and height.
- Interior surfaces must be smooth, durable, washable and impervious; unfinished wood and vermin-harboring seams/voids are not acceptable.
- Enclosed units require at least **74 inches clear aisle headroom** and **30 inches unobstructed aisle width**.
- Construction joints and penetrations must be tightly fitted, sealed, smooth and cleanable.
- Equipment/fixtures must be certified/listed as required (commonly NSF/ANSI for sanitation and UL or equivalent for electrical).
- Counter equipment generally needs 4 inches of unobstructed cleaning clearance unless readily movable under the stated exception.
- A second exit is required opposite the main exit or at the roof/rear, with an unobstructed opening of at least **24 × 36 inches**, openable from inside without a key/tool and labeled.
- All equipment, utensils, cylinders and loose items require positive travel restraint. Hot-liquid equipment needs secure lids or spill-containment compartments.
- A new MFF must be self-sustaining; design onboard water/waste capacity and an independent power source even if conditional utility connection may later be approved.
- All food storage/prep/display stays on the unit; plumbing and food-storage compartments must be separated.

### Service openings

- Project design: one architectural bar window/hatch, maximum concept size **60 × 30 inches**.
- The full architectural opening is not assumed to be an approved active food/beverage pass-through.
- Maximum two pass-through windows.

- Openings must be at least 18 inches apart.

### Handwashing and warewashing

Final requirements depend on menu/classification.

- When required, hand sink minimum: **9 × 9 × 5 inches**, readily accessible, with wall-mounted soap and paper-towel dispensers.

- Each drainboard must be at least the size of one basin, slope at least 1/8 inch per foot toward the basin, and have at least a 1/2-inch lip/rim.
- Provide a mixing faucet and swivel spout serving all warewashing basins.

### Water system

- Food-grade potable tank and plumbing certified by an ANSI-accredited organization.
- Tanks must be smooth inside, fully drainable, protected and securely mounted.
- Minimum water delivery: **1 gallon per minute to each sink basin**.
- Handwashing allocation: at least **5 gallons** when required.
- Warewashing allocation: add at least **15 gallons** for limited preparation or **25 gallons** beyond limited preparation, subject to final classification.
- Potable water used directly in beverages must be added to operational demand; size for the peak event plus reserve.
- Wastewater capacity: at least **150% of potable capacity**, never less than 7.5 gallons, plus applicable beverage-water and ice-melt allowances required by the reviewer.
- Waste tank requires watertight connections, shutoff/discharge valve and complete drainage.
- Removable potable or wastewater tanks may not exceed 10 gallons/80 pounds each under the SF guide.
- Potable fill and waste connections must be noninterchangeable, protected and clearly labeled.
- Water heater: at least 1/2 gallon or instantaneous to 100°F for handwashing-only systems; at least 4 gallons or instantaneous to 120°F when handwashing and warewashing are supplied.
- Provide shutoffs, strain relief, winterization/drain points, accessible pump/filter and a leak-containment strategy.

### Refrigeration, ventilation and fire

- Mechanically refrigerated storage is required for potentially hazardous foods/beverages; units must maintain required temperature during travel and service.
- Cooking/heat/grease-producing equipment may trigger a mechanical exhaust hood and fire-suppression requirements. Avoid adding it until operational need is proven.
- LP-gas equipment must be ANSI-certified and approved by the fire authority; cylinders must be secured to rigid structure.
- SF’s construction guide calls for at least a properly mounted **10 BC** extinguisher for gas-fired appliances; obtain SFFD’s project-specific extinguisher and suppression determination.
- Provide first-aid kit, protected lighting and safe, dry exit paths.
