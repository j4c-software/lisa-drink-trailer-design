# Lisa Drink Trailer — Locked Reference Trailer Photo and Plan Survey

**Revision:** 0.2 — September 7, 2026
**Status:** SUPPORTING EVIDENCE — NOT DESIGN AUTHORITY / NOT A FIELD SURVEY
**Reference:** 1989 Circle J two-horse straight-load bumper-pull, Horse Trailer Trader stock 54738
**Purpose:** Record observable shell, running-gear, opening and structural cues for the provisional Phase 1 model without misrepresenting raster photographs as measured donor geometry

## 1. Use and limitations

The locked reference listing states a steel, tandem-axle, two-horse straight-load bumper-pull trailer with listed dimensions of 10 ft long × 5 ft wide × 6.8 ft high. The five listing photographs are the approved visual proxy for representative body form and proportion until a donor trailer is acquired.

This survey separates:

- **LISTED** facts stated by the reference listing;
- **OBSERVED** features directly visible in one or more photographs;
- **PHOTO-ESTIMATED** proportions scaled from the listed dimensions; and
- **UNKNOWN** geometry that the photographs cannot support.

Perspective, lens distortion, low resolution, occlusion and unknown measurement conventions materially limit the estimates. No value in this survey is field-verified. Photo-estimated values may guide a provisional visualization layer, but they may not support purchase acceptance, equipment fit, structural design, cutting, fabrication or permitting.

Third-party photographs and the secondary Pinterest plan were inspected from their original URLs. They are not copied into the repository or approved for presentation reuse.

## 4. Photo 001 — curb/passenger-side profile

### Direct observations

- The visible side is the side without the forward personnel/escape door; it is the best reference for the proposed curb/customer wall.
- The front body is not square in plan. The side panel rolls into a rounded front corner before meeting the broad front face.
- The colored roof cap is a distinct assembly above the steel wall, separated by a continuous eave/trim seam.
- The roof cap has rounded front and rear corners and a shallow crown. Its exact transverse radius and construction cannot be measured.
- Three upper glazed/opening zones are visible: a small opening wrapping the front corner, a second small forward-side window and one large rear-side barred/sliding window.
- The large rear-side window occupies much of the rear half of the wall and approaches the rear portal.
- Multiple continuous horizontal rails/seams wrap the body below the roof and around the mid-body stripe zone.
- Vertical seams/posts are visible at the front transition, around window/opening jambs and at the rear portal.
- The tandem axle group is concentrated in the rear half of the 120-inch body.
- The rear tire sits close to the rear body plane. The shared fender begins ahead of the front tire and visually terminates at or very near the rear body plane.
- Both tires appear similar in diameter and ride height. Exact tire size, width, load range and wheel specification are unreadable.
- The body/floor frame sits above the tire-center line; the wheel arches/fender occupy a substantial portion of the lower wall height.

## 5. Photo 002 — street/driver-side oblique view

### Direct observations

- A forward side/escape door exists on this side of the representative trailer.
- The door is forward of the large barred/sliding horse window and appears to occupy or overlap the forward work-zone vicinity.
- The door leaf is open in the photograph, which makes apparent width and longitudinal placement unreliable.
- The door includes a small upper window and substantial perimeter framing/hinge hardware.
- A large upper barred/sliding window occupies the wall behind the door.
- The upper and mid-body horizontal rails continue across or around the door/window regions.
- The rear portal/door edge is visible at the far right and appears structurally heavier than the intermediate wall framing.
- The shared fender rises immediately below the large window region.

### Conversion implications

- Options B and C must treat the retained street-side door as a real interruption, not a small symbolic notch in a continuous backbar.
- The door swing, latch, threshold, step, grab handle and interior release need their own operating and egress volumes.
- The photograph cannot establish whether the door falls wholly inside the 24-inch front support zone, crosses the 24/90-inch boundary or occupies the main work zone. That position remains a candidate-measurement priority.
- Option A's permanent infill assumption requires a measured door/frame map and engineered replacement load path; the photo does not support a flush cosmetic infill alone.

### Photo-scaled door envelope

Mapping the visible street-side wall to the listed 120-inch body suggests an outer door-frame envelope from approximately X 15 to X 38 inches aft of the front body datum. The corresponding visible envelope is approximately 22 ±4 inches wide and 72 ±4 inches high. The small upper door-window aperture appears approximately 5–7 inches wide × 9–11 inches high.

These are deliberately low-confidence outer-envelope estimates from a highly oblique image. They do not establish clear opening, rough opening, threshold height, frame section, door thickness or swing clearance. The estimate suggests that the door likely crosses the canonical X = 24-inch front-nook/work-zone boundary, making it a direct constraint for Options B and C.

## 6. Photo 003 — front three-quarter view

### Direct observations

- The trailer has a broad, nearly flat front face with large rounded transitions into both sidewalls.
- It is not a wedge/V-nose body and should not be represented as a rectangular box with sharp front corners.
- The lower front wall also carries rounded corners rather than terminating in square vertical edges.
- Two elongated rounded-rectangle windows occupy the upper front face. Additional small side windows wrap near the corners.
- A central vertical sheet seam or framing line is visible on the front face.
- Horizontal belt/trim rails wrap continuously around the rounded front corners.
- The fiberglass roof cap is shallowly crowned across the width, with large rounded front corners and a small front overhang/eave.
- The roof cap and steel wall are separate assemblies; front curvature should not imply a monolithic molded shell.
- The A-frame converges ahead of the body and attaches below/behind the front wall plane. Its rail section and underbody continuation are not visible.
- The visible side door/window/spare-tire relationships confirm that the rounded front consumes some nominal rectangular interior volume near both forward corners.

### Model consequence

The provisional plan should use a broad front face plus rounded corner transitions. The corner radius/depth must remain an unconstrained visual proxy until a candidate supplies a plan profile. A semicircular nose, pointed nose or sharp rectangular front would all contradict the reference.

## 7. Photo 004 — rear with doors closed

### Direct observations

- The rear consists of two approximately equal hinged door leaves meeting at the centerline.
- Each door has an open/barred upper zone and solid lower panel.
- Hinges are outboard; latching/overlap hardware is concentrated at the center and lower portions.
- The rear portal has rounded upper corners following the roof cap.
- The rear roof cap overhangs/rounds into the portal rather than ending as a sharp square edge.
- Tail/marker lights, reflectors and the license plate occupy the rear wall and lower corner zones.
- The side fenders project beyond the nominal 60-inch body width on both sides.
- A spare tire mounted along one side creates a separate travel-width/clearance projection that is not included in a simple body-only plan.
- The lower rear corners/fender terminations are shaped and tapered; they should not be treated as full rectangular body corners at running-gear height.

### Photo-scaled proportion

Scaling the apparent outer fender width against the apparent 60-inch body width gives an approximate 78-inch total width over the fenders. Perspective and the spare tire prevent a more precise result. The Master Design Spec's 84-inch provisional maximum remains the correct clearance-planning input.

Using the listed 60-inch body width and 81.6-inch body height as separate scale checks, the visible rear portal is approximately 50 inches wide × 78 inches high, with working uncertainty of approximately ±3 inches in either direction. Each closed door leaf presents an approximately 25-inch-wide × 78-inch-high face, with approximately ±2 inches width and ±3 inches height uncertainty. Center overlap, perimeter stops, seals, hinges and latch hardware mean neither visible envelope is a clear-opening dimension.

### Rear-opening caution

The photo estimate supports the current conservative 48 × 72-inch provisional clear opening but does not verify it. Perspective, portal thickness, door overlap and unclear measurement conventions prevent a defensible clear-opening dimension. Retain 48 × 72 inches from the Master Design Spec until the candidate is measured.

## 8. Photo 005 — rear with doors open

### Direct observations

- Both rear leaves swing outward and require exterior sweep/hold-open clearance.
- A center divider separates two straight-load stalls and runs longitudinally toward the front.
- The divider and original stall hardware are removal/demolition items, not usable conversion structure unless inspected and deliberately retained.
- Dark lower kick panels line both sides and conceal portions of the lower wall/wheel-well region.
- Rubber floor mats and a central floor seam obscure the original floor material and cross-members.
- Multiple transverse roof bows are visible. Their exact count and longitudinal centers cannot be measured reliably from the photograph.
- Sidewall posts/rails and the roof-to-wall connection are visible but partly concealed by liners and original horse equipment.
- Front and side windows provide cross-shell alignment cues, but no reliable dimensions.
- Interior wheel-well geometry is not sufficiently visible to infer length, projection or height.
- The rear portal appears to be a primary body-stiffening frame and must be preserved unless an engineer directs otherwise.

### Conversion implications

- The rear-door swing volume must be modeled outside the shell as loose operating clearance, especially where furniture, utility service or queue elements might be placed.
- Removal of stall partitions and liners is necessary before a complete structural map can be created.
- No underfloor tank or cabinet geometry should be placed from these photographs because the frame/cross-members and wheel wells remain concealed.

### Front-curvature comparison

- The Pinterest trailer uses a continuous circular front arc with a labeled radius and a measurable nose depth.
- The locked Circle J photographs instead show a broad, nearly flat front face joined to the sidewalls by rounded corner transitions.
- These are different plan-profile families. Applying the Pinterest R875 arc directly would turn the Circle J proxy into a different trailer and would overstate the loss of forward interior floor area.
- The appropriate lesson is methodological: parameterize the Circle J with front-face width, corner tangency points and corner radius or control offsets. Keep all values unset until a candidate plan-profile survey is available.
- The current Phase 1 drawing therefore retains the broad-face/rounded-corner form supported by Circle J photos 001 and 003. The Pinterest curve is recorded as a non-governing alternate precedent, not blended into the reference geometry.

### Running-gear comparison

The Pinterest drawing cannot check wheel placement. It shows a side-mounted spare tire but omits the road wheels, fenders, axle centerlines, track width and wheel wells. The current approximately 68-inch and 100-inch axle centers, approximately 32-inch spacing and approximately 54–120-inch fender envelope remain derived only from Circle J listing photos 001 and 004.

## 11. Critical next measurements

The photo walk-around increases the priority of these candidate checks:

1. Body length measurement convention: frontmost curved face to rear portal, excluding all hitch hardware.
2. Cross-body plan profile at floor, 36-inch counter, belt rail and roof-spring heights.
3. Front-corner tangency points and offsets from a rectangular 120 × 60-inch bounding box.
4. Tongue projection from front body datum to coupler center and full A-frame rail geometry.
5. Both axle centerlines from front and rear body datums.
6. Axle spacing, tire outside diameter, tire width, track width and hub-face dimension.
7. Fender leading/trailing edges, maximum projection and clearance to full tire travel.
8. Interior wheel-well length, height, projection and relation to axle centers.
9. Street-side door jambs from front body/floor datums, clear opening and full swing.
10. Every curb-side window/opening jamb from front and floor datums.
11. Wall posts, belt rails, headers, roof bows and rear-portal sections.
12. Rear-door portal and true clear opening with both doors secured open.
13. Spare-tire and any other removable projection included in travel width.
14. Body height versus road height, including frame, roof cap, vents and future rooftop equipment.

## 12. Phase 1 modeling controls resulting from this survey

- Keep separate layers for `REFERENCE PHOTO ESTIMATE`, `DESIGN CLEARANCE ENVELOPE` and future `CANDIDATE MEASURED` geometry.
- The body bounding box remains 120 × 60 inches, but its rounded front corners remove usable area from that rectangle.
- Use axle centers at X ≈ 68 and X ≈ 100 inches only in the reference-estimate layer.
- Use approximately 29-inch tire outside diameter only as a visible reference proxy.
- Show approximately 78 inches over reference fenders while retaining the independent 84-inch maximum planning envelope.
- Do not create internal wheel wells from the fender outline.
- Keep the street-side door, roof bows, wall posts and underfloor frame as unresolved/no-fit zones until measured.
- Keep the 48-inch tongue as the conservative design assumption, but annotate that the reference photo appears shorter.
- Keep the Pinterest R875/420 mm nose construction out of the Circle J reference layer; retain it only as a clearly named alternate precedent for future comparison.
- Any candidate fit evaluation must replace photo-derived axle, fender, tire, tongue and opening estimates with supplied measurements before classification.

## 13. Survey conclusion

The five reference photographs are adequate to establish the representative visual form: broad rounded front, shallow crowned roof cap, tandem axles concentrated toward the rear, a long shared fender, one forward street-side escape door, large upper side openings and rear double doors.

They are not adequate to establish internal wheel wells, structural-member positions, exact door/window openings, roof radii, frame geometry or fabrication dimensions. The Phase 1 model may now show a clearly labeled reference running-gear layer, but those objects must remain replaceable parameters with visible uncertainty and hard downstream holds.

The secondary Pinterest plan adds a valuable parametric nose precedent and several original horse-layout details, but it is a different, longer and wider trailer. It neither validates the Circle J wheel placement nor supplies donor dimensions.
